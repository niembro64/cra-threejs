# Eric Niemeyer — Teaching, Operations, and Organizational Leadership

## Daegu Science High School

Eric taught STEM subjects in English to gifted Korean high-school students preparing for advanced science and engineering education. He designed active lessons that combined technical concepts, planning, and English communication, and built an English and Hangul scheduling application for faculty and student lookups. He also participated in assemblies, teacher and parent conferences, curriculum planning, and mentorship of other teachers.

## Assessment and classroom judgment

Eric evaluated English ability holistically rather than reducing it to exact grammar. He considered clarity, command of the language, willingness and ability to communicate, writing needs, and likely real-world use. When a student disrupted a class, he used available local teaching support when necessary and kept the main lesson moving rather than allowing one incident to consume the group.

## A.C.L.E. programs in Italy and the United Kingdom

Eric taught English and pedagogy, supported office and training-camp technology, trained other teachers, adapted curricula, and helped operate camps across multiple cities. Camps combined grammar, sports, drama, skits, and performances for groups ranging from small programs to large groups of children. Weather, parent expectations, lesson changes, or an absent performer could force rapid replanning. He also led a student excursion through the United Kingdom, coordinating travel, meals, transportation, routines, and safety.

## National Ultimate league operations

As executive manager of Republic of Korea Ultimate, Eric coordinated a national mixed-gender sports league spanning regional leaders, a board, captains, venues, sponsors, uniforms, equipment, budgets, and many teams. He ran captain and board meetings, handled venue and weather changes, tracked action items, navigated disagreements, and mentored his successor on technology, money, executive responsibilities, and stakeholder relationships.

## Fairness, safety, and ethics

League leadership required judgment where written rules did not answer every question. Eric avoided retroactive punishment when playing-time expectations had not been clearly established, then used direct questions with captains to surface how they planned to include less experienced players. He also treated participant safety and responsible event boundaries as leadership duties even when the surrounding culture or local rules were permissive.

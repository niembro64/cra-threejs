# Eric Niemeyer — Rockwell Automation and Maverick Technologies

## Manufacturing execution systems

At Rockwell Automation and Maverick Technologies, Eric designed and implemented manufacturing execution system software for large industrial clients. MES sits between plant-floor activity and business systems, so his work connected PLCs, robots, equipment state, operator HMIs, databases, traceability records, and ERP-level workflows. The core challenge was making software reflect the real production process reliably enough for people and machines to use it.

## Lucid Motors automotive integration

At Lucid Motors in Casa Grande, Eric worked onsite across MES integration, robotics and PLC coordination, business-layer data, and track-and-trace behavior. Automotive production involved many stakeholders across bays and companies, including machining, controls, quality, business systems, and client leadership. Successful delivery depended on discovering the actual process and coordinating boundaries between systems rather than treating the project as an isolated web application.

A representative integration began with identifying or scanning a part, selecting the correct bay-level operation, sending instructions to a torque tool, receiving the actual completion value, persisting traceability, and presenting the result for operator confirmation. Eric coordinated PLC engineers, QA engineers, operators, application and database teams, and ERP stakeholders while respecting the boundary between MES software and direct machine control.

For a Lucid torque-tool workflow, Eric diagrammed the proposed HMI and navigation before coding, presented it to client and project leadership, obtained written approval, and then implemented the approved interface. The workflow left a clear trail from requirement through design decision to delivered software.

## Controlled-material traceability

For 3M, Eric led or managed integration around an evidence and material incineration workflow. The process demanded strict traceability and awareness of chemical and operational requirements. The software needed to preserve a trustworthy record of what moved through the process and how physical operations corresponded to digital state.

When a separate 3M engagement accumulated rapidly changing requirements and ultimately ended, Eric's timestamped OneNote notes, meeting minutes, decisions, and action items provided an objective account of requested work and completed effort. Those records supported an evidence-based termination negotiation. During part of this work, he balanced 3M project leadership with onsite Lucid engineering by protecting calendar buffers, tracking time separately, and arranging backup ownership for unavoidable overlaps.

## Tire and food manufacturing

For Continental Tire, Eric contributed Java and operator-interface work. At Cooper Tire, he documented manufacturing processes onsite so new MES behavior could map onto existing equipment and workflows. For Maple Leaf and Green Leaf food-manufacturing work, he handled backend, database, and testing responsibilities in environments where older machines and reproducible process behavior imposed real constraints.

## Cross-team engineering and mentorship

Eric translated requirements for offshore developers, tested delivered code, fixed implementation issues himself when necessary, and taught practical VBA and Excel tooling. He also mentored newer Rockwell engineers. His role frequently bridged client subject-matter experts, PLC and controls owners, database and application developers, quality teams, and management.

## Requirements and production risk

Industrial changes could arrive inside agile or waterfall delivery. In agile work, Eric used Jira and sprint planning to sequence changes. In waterfall work, he documented the revised agreement, assumptions, and schedule impact. He used tact with controls owners who were understandably protective of production PLCs, recognizing that an integration request can feel like surrendering control of a safety- or revenue-critical system.
